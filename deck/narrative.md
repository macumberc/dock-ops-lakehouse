# Meridian Freight presentation narrative

The audience is a VP of Terminal Operations and a Data/Platform Architect. The
presentation asks them to sponsor a scoped Memphis pilot, assign accountable
owners, and schedule a kickoff that approves the baseline, scorecard, and cost cap.

The first five slides establish the use case before introducing the software:
a fictional LTL carrier has 36 dock doors across three terminals, arrival waves
outgrow a fixed roster, and waiting trailers create detention and modeled
overtime. Memphis has the largest terminal cost and becomes the recurring
example. The desired change is a supervisor-approved staffing decision before
the arrival peak.

The demonstration follows that decision. The actual operations screen connects
the door snapshot to a dated staffing gap. A Memphis forecast row needs 17
workers against 8 scheduled, so the supervisor reviews a nine-worker gap.
The AI briefing states the action, and Genie answers a leadership follow-up from
the same gold data. The architecture slide then explains the data path the
audience has just seen.

The proposal distinguishes working prototype evidence from proposed pilot
acceptance. It names weekly owners and exit gates, uses Memphis-specific
economics aligned to the proposed 10% detention reduction, and closes with actions due within two and five business days plus a
45-minute kickoff. Suggested targets remain explicitly proposed, and gross
benefit remains an illustrative sensitivity until finance validates a real baseline.

Visual direction: large Barlow typography, restrained Databricks color, generous
spacing, a sourced dock photograph, a verified app screenshot, editable charts
and tables, and original product icons from the local logo library. Images of
the app show the real deployed demonstration. The cover photograph illustrates
the setting and does not depict the fictional company.

Twelve main slides support 19 minutes of prepared delivery and six minutes of
discussion. Two appendices support technical questions and presenter navigation.
Every main slide has a lead-in, a specific proof or demonstration, a business
implication, and a transition to the next part of the story.
